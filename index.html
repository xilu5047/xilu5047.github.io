Research interests

I am interested in the conditions under which meaningful causal structure can be recovered from observed data, particularly when the underlying variables are latent and observations come from different environments.

PRIMARY RESEARCH DIRECTION

Causal Representation Learning from Multiple Distributions

Cross-environment changes can provide information that is unavailable from a single distribution. I study how such changes constrain latent representations and what assumptions are sufficient for recovering interpretable causal structure. My interests include the identifiability of latent Markov or moral graphs, weaker sufficient-change conditions, and limitations of observational recovery.

Multi-environment learningLatent graph identifiabilityDistribution shifts
R.01 / THEORY

Structural identifiability

When does the information carried by heterogeneous distributions suffice to determine properties of a shared latent graph, even if individual latent variables remain ambiguous?

R.02 / ENVIRONMENTS

Necessary variation across distributions

How can sufficient-change assumptions be weakened, and what types or amounts of cross-environment variation are required for structural recovery?

R.03 / PARTIAL DATA

Learning with incomplete observations

How do incomplete and informatively missing observations affect the identifiability of latent relationships across environments?

These are current research questions and directions, not claims of published results.
